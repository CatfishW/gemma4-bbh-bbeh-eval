# VOLT: Variance-Optimal aLlocation of Tokens for Group-Relative RLVR

Working method note. This document states the estimator, the allocation rule, the
propositions that justify them, and the algorithm actually implemented in `rl/`.

## 1. Setting and notation

Training pool of prompts `x_1..x_M` (the frozen calibration rows, index < 25, of
BBH / BBEH / USR tasks). Policy `pi_theta` (Gemma 4 E2B + LoRA adapter). A rollout
`y ~ pi_theta(.|x)` receives a binary verifiable reward `r(x, y) in {0, 1}` computed
by the repository scorer (`eval_benchmarks.evaluate_correctness`), identical to the
frozen evaluation protocol.

Per-prompt success rate under the current policy:

    p_i(theta) = E_{y ~ pi_theta(.|x_i)} [ r(x_i, y) ]

Primary objective `J(theta) = (1/M) sum_i p_i(theta)`, with a deployment-efficiency
constraint `E[len(y)] <= L_target` handled by a Lagrangian dual (Section 6).

Score function `S_i(y) = grad_theta log pi_theta(y|x_i)`. Policy gradient per prompt:

    g_i = grad p_i = E[ r S_i ] = E[ (r - b) S_i ]   for any y-independent baseline b.

## 2. Where gradient signal lives (why allocation matters)

**Proposition 1 (signal density).** For binary rewards and baseline `b = p_i`:

    || g_i ||  <=  sqrt( p_i (1 - p_i) ) * sqrt( E || S_i ||^2 )        (Cauchy-Schwarz)

and the per-rollout estimator `(r - p_i) S_i` has second moment

    E || (r - p_i) S_i ||^2  ~=  p_i (1 - p_i) * E || S_i ||^2

under the standard weak-correlation approximation between `r` and `||S||^2`
(Greensmith et al., 2004). Both the true gradient magnitude and the per-rollout
information carried about it scale with the Bernoulli variance `p_i (1 - p_i)`.
Prompts the current policy always solves (`p ~ 1`) or never solves (`p ~ 0`)
contribute essentially zero gradient. This is the continuous form of the
"degenerate group" observation motivating DAPO's dynamic sampling: a GRPO group
that is all-correct or all-wrong yields exactly zero advantage. Uniform group
sizes therefore waste generated tokens exactly where learning cannot happen.

**Proposition 2 (variance-optimal token allocation).** Consider one update built
from per-prompt averaged estimators `g_hat_i` with `n_i` rollouts each, uniform
prompt weighting, and per-rollout token cost `l_i` (mean completion length). The
total estimator variance is `sum_i v_i / n_i` with `v_i = Var[(r - b_i) S_i]`.
Minimizing it subject to the generation budget `sum_i n_i l_i <= B` gives the
square-root allocation

    n_i*  proportional to  sqrt( v_i / l_i ).

With `v_i ~= p_i (1 - p_i) * kappa * l_i` (score-norm second moment scales
approximately linearly with sequence length: it is a sum over tokens), the length
factors cancel and

    n_i*  proportional to  sqrt( p_i (1 - p_i) ).

The same `n_i*` maximizes the standard one-step expected-improvement lower bound
`eta <grad J, E Delta> - (L eta^2 / 2) E || Delta ||^2`, because the first term is
allocation-invariant for any unbiased estimator (Proposition 3) and the second is
the variance being minimized. VOLT therefore water-fills rollouts on
`sqrt(E[p_i (1 - p_i)])` computed under the posterior of Section 4, subject to
box constraints `n_i in {0} union [1, n_max]` and an exploration floor
(Section 5).

## 3. Predictable baselines: unbiased signal from a single rollout

GRPO's advantage uses the within-group empirical mean (and std): the baseline for
sample `t` *includes* `r_t` itself, which (i) scales the expected gradient by
`(1 - 1/n)` for binary rewards, (ii) is undefined for `n = 1`, (iii) is
identically zero for degenerate groups, and (iv) after std-division, distorts
weighting across difficulty levels (Dr. GRPO). RLOO's leave-one-out baseline fixes
(i) but still requires `n >= 2` and still yields exactly zero signal on degenerate
groups, because a within-group baseline has no way to distinguish "this prompt is
easy" from "this group got lucky".

VOLT replaces the within-group baseline with a **predictable** (filtration-
measurable) baseline: the discounted posterior mean of `p_i` computed from
*previous* iterations only.

**Proposition 3 (unbiasedness under adaptive allocation).** Let `F_k` be the
sigma-algebra generated by everything sampled up to iteration `k`. Let the
allocation rule (which prompts, how many rollouts) be any `F_{k-1}`-measurable
policy, and let the baseline `b_{i,k}` be `F_{k-1}`-measurable. Then for every
rollout `y` drawn at iteration `k` from prompt `i`:

    E[ (r(y) - b_{i,k}) S_i(y) | F_{k-1} ]  =  g_i(theta_k),

so the batch update direction satisfies

    E[ Delta_k ]  =  E[ sum_i n_{i,k} g_i(theta_k) ],

an adaptively re-weighted combination of true per-prompt gradients with
non-negative predictable weights. No optional-stopping bias is introduced by
difficulty-adaptive sampling, because each increment is a martingale-difference
correction around a predictable baseline. (GRPO's group-mean baseline is *not*
predictable — it looks at the current group — which is exactly why it needs
`n >= 2` and dies on degenerate groups.)

Two practical consequences:

1. `n_i = 1` is admissible: even a single rollout carries unbiased signal
   `(r - b_i) S_i`, so the allocator is free to spread probe rollouts widely.
2. An all-correct group on a prompt with posterior mean `b_i < 1` still yields a
   *positive* advantage `1 - b_i > 0` on every sample: consolidation of fragile
   successes, with a weight that automatically anneals to zero as the posterior
   approaches certainty. Symmetrically, all-wrong groups on prompts with
   `b_i > 0` push probability away from failure modes. GRPO discards both;
   DAPO resamples and *filters* both, spending tokens it then throws away.

## 4. Difficulty tracking: discounted hierarchical Beta posteriors

Per prompt `i`, VOLT maintains a discounted Beta posterior over `p_i`:

    alpha_i <- gamma * alpha_i + wins_k,      beta_i <- gamma * beta_i + losses_k,

with discount `gamma in (0, 1)` applied once per iteration in which the prompt is
sampled (non-stationarity tracking: the policy drifts, so old outcomes decay; the
effective sample size is bounded by `ESS <= n_bar / (1 - gamma)`).

The prior for a prompt with no (or decayed) observations comes from a task-level
hyperprior (partial pooling over the benchmark's task structure):

    prior_i = Beta( m * mu_tau(i) + 1,  m * (1 - mu_tau(i)) + 1 ),

where `mu_tau` is the discounted success mean pooled over all prompts of task
`tau` and `m` is the prior strength. Task pooling matters here because BBH / BBEH
/ USR difficulty is strongly task-structured (the repository's CBRR router
exploits exactly this structure; VOLT inherits it as a prior rather than as a
routing decision).

Posterior quantities used elsewhere:

    b_i     = E[p_i]         = alpha' / (alpha' + beta')                (baseline)
    u_i     = E[p_i (1-p_i)] = alpha' beta' / ((alpha'+beta')^2 (alpha'+beta'+1))
                              + Var[p_i]  ... computed exactly as
              E[p(1-p)] = alpha' beta' / ( (alpha'+beta') (alpha'+beta'+1) )
    s_i     = sqrt(u_i)                                                 (allocation score)

where `alpha', beta'` include the hierarchical prior. Note `u_i` is the posterior
*expectation* of the Bernoulli variance, which is optimistic for uncertain
prompts (a fresh prompt has `u_i` near its task prior, not near zero) — an
information-directed exploration bonus arises naturally, with no extra tuning.

## 5. The VOLT iteration

Budget: `N_k` rollouts (or a token budget `B_k`) per iteration.

1. **Freeze** posterior state -> baselines `b_i`, scores `s_i`, length EMAs.
2. **Allocate**: `n_i = round(N_k * s_i / sum_j s_j)` clipped to `[0, n_max]`,
   with an exploration floor: a reserved fraction `eps_floor` of `N_k` is
   distributed round-robin to the least-recently-sampled prompts (guarantees
   every prompt is revisited; keeps posteriors live).
3. **Generate** rollouts batched at temperature `T`, chat template identical to
   the deployed evaluation (single user message, no system message).
4. **Score** with the repository scorer -> `r in {0,1}`; record lengths.
5. **Advantages**: `A = r_tilde - b_tilde_i` (shaped reward and its predictable
   baseline, Section 6), broadcast to tokens.
6. **Update** LoRA parameters with the clipped token-level policy-gradient loss
   and a constant length normalizer (Dr. GRPO style, no per-group std division,
   no length-biased token averaging), single pass over the iteration's data.
7. **Update** posteriors, task hyperpriors, length EMAs, and the dual `lambda`.

GRPO is recovered as a special case (mode `grpo`): fixed `n_i = G`, within-group
mean/std advantages, no posteriors — same loss, same sampling code, same scorer,
enabling a controlled comparison.

## 6. Success-conditioned length shaping (deployment efficiency)

Constrained objective: `max J(theta)` s.t. `E[len | correct] <= L_target`.
Shaped reward applies the length penalty **only to correct rollouts**:

    r_tilde = r * (1 - lambda * min(1, len / L_max))

Penalizing wrong rollouts for length would reward "give up early" behavior;
penalizing only successes steers probability mass toward the *shortest working*
solutions, which is the deployment quantity that matters (the repository reports
mean completion tokens per accuracy point). The multiplier `lambda >= 0` follows
projected dual ascent on the moving average of correct-rollout lengths:

    lambda <- clip( lambda + eta_lambda * ( len_bar_correct / L_target - 1 ),  0, lambda_max ).

The baseline for the shaped reward stays predictable: `b_tilde_i = b_i * (1 -
lambda * min(1, lbar_i / L_max))` with `lbar_i` the frozen length EMA of prompt
i's correct rollouts (falling back to the task EMA).

## 7. Evaluation protocol (pre-committed)

- **Train**: calibration rows only (`index < 25`), all 60+ tasks, except a
  held-out task subset (every 4th task per benchmark, alphabetical) excluded from
  training entirely to measure unseen-task generalization.
- **Model selection**: validation rows (`25 <= index < 50`) only.
- **Final claims**: frozen test rows (`index >= 50`), greedy decoding, prompts
  `direct_answer` and `concise_cot`, scored by the unchanged repository scorer —
  directly comparable to the published `direct_answer` 26.39%,
  `concise_cot_self_rank_k3` 35.06%, and `cbrr_policy` 35.41% rows.
- **Efficiency claims**: (i) training efficiency: validation accuracy as a
  function of cumulative *generated tokens*, VOLT vs GRPO at matched budgets;
  (ii) deployment efficiency: mean completion tokens at matched accuracy.
- **Baselines**: GRPO (G=8, std-normalized), Dr. GRPO variant (no std), and
  ablations: VOLT without allocation (uniform n_i, predictable baseline only),
  VOLT without hierarchy (flat Beta(1,1) prior), VOLT without length shaping.

## 8. Relation to prior work (verified against the literature through mid-2026)

The three ingredients are individually adjacent to published work; the paper's
claim is the unification — one hierarchically-pooled Bayesian state that drives
allocation, provides the predictable baseline, and modulates the budget dual —
plus the optional-stopping unbiasedness theorem that no prior allocation work
establishes.

**Group-relative RLVR and its estimator fixes.**
- GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) fixed-size groups, group-mean/std advantages; degenerate
  groups carry zero gradient.
- DAPO (arXiv:2503.14476) names the degenerate-group problem and patches it by
  rejection: oversample, discard degenerate groups. SPO later derived the
  hyperbolic resampling cost E[N | p] = 1/(p(1-p)) - 1. Filtering spends the
  tokens it throws away; VOLT never generates them.
- Dr. GRPO (arXiv:2503.20783) removes std-division and length-biased
  normalization; VOLT adopts both fixes.
- RLOO (arXiv:2402.14740; Kool et al. 2019) leave-one-out baselines: unbiased
  but within-group (n >= 2, degenerate groups still dead).
- SPO (arXiv:2509.13232) is the closest baseline work: a persistent
  KL-discounted Beta tracker whose pre-update mean serves as an
  action-independent baseline, giving group-free single-rollout signal. VOLT
  differs in (i) the martingale analysis under *adaptive allocation* (SPO
  argues unbiasedness only for fixed sampling), (ii) hierarchical partial
  pooling across benchmark tasks (SPO tracks prompts independently), and
  (iii) coupling the same state to a variance-optimal token allocator.
- KRPO (arXiv:2505.07527) Kalman baselines; A*-PO (arXiv:2505.20686) offline
  V* baseline; OPO (arXiv:2505.23585) optimal length-weighted baseline;
  shrinkage baselines (arXiv:2511.03710) James-Stein toward the batch mean.
  None address adaptive rollout counts.

**Adaptive rollout allocation.**
- GRESO (arXiv:2506.02177) skips predicted zero-variance prompts (binary
  keep/skip); SPEED-RL (arXiv:2506.09016) SNR-based prompt screening;
  MoPPS (arXiv:2507.04632) and BOTS (arXiv:2510.26374) Beta-posterior Thompson
  sampling for *selection* — none size groups per prompt.
- DynaMO (arXiv:2602.19208) derives water-level allocation n_i ∝ sigma_i with
  frequentist Bernoulli-variance estimates: the allocation exponent of VOLT,
  without cost normalization, posteriors, pooling, or baseline changes.
- Knapsack-RL (arXiv:2509.25849) value/cost knapsack with stale per-epoch
  success estimates; token-length cost named as future work.
- DUET (arXiv:2605.08441) cost-weighted Neyman allocation n_q ∝ sigma_q /
  sqrt(lambda L_q) with a training-token budget dual. VOLT's rule n_i ∝
  sqrt(p_i(1-p_i)) is the same constrained program under a different variance
  model: DUET treats per-rollout variance as length-independent, while VOLT
  models the score-norm second moment as growing linearly with length
  (a sum over token scores), which cancels the cost denominator. The variance
  model is stated explicitly so the disagreement is a testable assumption, not
  an algebraic slip.
- Reinforce-Ada (arXiv:2510.04996) successive-elimination sequential sampling
  with a pooled-mean baseline; its own stopping rule's interaction with the
  baseline is left unanalyzed — exactly the gap Proposition 3 closes.
- SARA (arXiv:2607.26253) SPRT-style sequential budget commitment with Beta
  posteriors (credibility caveats); HORA (arXiv:2605.07114) posterior
  hit-utility reallocation for pass@k; VIGOR (arXiv:2607.22002) greedy
  variance-seeking regeneration; AR3PO (arXiv:2509.25808) adaptive rollout
  with response reuse; DARS (arXiv:2508.13755) difficulty re-weighting.

**Budget/length control.**
- Leash (arXiv:2512.21540) primal-dual dual ascent on E[length]; ALP
  (arXiv:2506.05256) difficulty-modulated penalties; IBPO (arXiv:2501.17974)
  constrained-utility framing; L1/LCPO (arXiv:2503.04697) prompt-specified
  budgets and the accuracy-vs-token evaluation protocol; Kimi k1.5
  (arXiv:2501.12599) min-max length reward on correct rollouts; ThinkPrune
  (arXiv:2504.01296); GFPO (arXiv:2508.09726). VOLT's shaping is
  success-conditioned dual ascent — the component is deliberately standard;
  what is new is that the dual controller reads the same posterior state as
  the allocator.

**Classical theory.** Optimal constant baselines (Weaver & Tao, UAI 2001),
variance analysis of gradient estimators (Greensmith, Bartlett & Baxter, JMLR
2004), REINFORCE (Williams 1992). VOLT's allocation program is the classical
variance-minimization water-filling applied at the prompt level with token
costs; the predictable-baseline construction is the standard martingale trick
from sequential analysis, apparently not previously applied to make adaptive
rollout allocation unbiased in RLVR.

**Hierarchical difficulty pooling.** IRT and hierarchical Bayesian difficulty
models are used offline for evaluation and routing (IrtNet arXiv:2510.00844,
IRT-Router ACL 2025, RADAR arXiv:2509.25426) but, per a dedicated search, not
inside an RLVR training loop; task-level partial pooling of prompt difficulty
appears novel there.

## 9. Failure modes and controls

- **Reward hacking of the scorer**: the scorer is frozen and identical to the
  deployed protocol; qualitative samples are logged every iteration.
- **Posterior staleness**: discounting bounds ESS; the exploration floor bounds
  the revisit interval; both are ablated.
- **Allocation collapse** (all budget on a few prompts): n_max cap and floor
  prevent it; allocation entropy is logged.
- **Optimizing to the calibration rows** (overfitting 1.5k prompts): held-out
  validation early stopping; unseen-task subset quantifies transfer; final test
  is single-shot.
